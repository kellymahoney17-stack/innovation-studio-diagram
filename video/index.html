One object, ten panels

Nothing new is ever drawn. The same marks become Marcus's eleven years of giving, separate into two, go quiet, spread from here to there, resolve into a picture of him, extend into a practice, adjust as he works it, and land inside the console. Every mark on screen at the end was on screen at the start. The argument is in the movement rather than in any one frame, so it is worth running the whole thing through before reading a single panel.

How to read it

    
The piece runs on a few fixed rules. Each is a constraint on the animation, not a description of the product.


    
The continuity ruleThirty six marks are created once, on load, and only ever repositioned. No panel introduces a new element. If a panel needs something that is not already on screen, the panel is wrong.


    
The apparatusThe dashed rules, anchors and spans are the product doing its work. They appear only in the panels where something is being organized, which is why panel 05 has none. Nothing is being organized there.


    
Two colors, and only two Marcus and  his wife. This is the only second color in the piece, because panel 03 turns on being able to tell the two apart, and panel 04 turns on their staying apart. The marks keep those origins through to the end.


    
PacingA panel holds for as long as its narration takes to read aloud, so the clock is computed from the script rather than hand set, and moves when the script does. Pace, lead in, travel and the easing curve are all editable under Timing above, and any panel can be given a fixed hold that overrides the derived one.


    
Status of the copyThe narration is a voice over draft and stands in for the real thing. Donor wording is Johny's call.
